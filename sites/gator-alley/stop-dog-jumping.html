How to Stop Your Dog Jumping on People

A dog who launches at every guest is not being rude, they are excited and it has worked before. The fix is teaching what to do instead and being consistent about it.

Why dogs jump

Jumping is a greeting. A dog jumps to get closer to a face, and any reaction, even pushing the dog away or saying no, counts as attention. If it ever worked, it will be repeated.

What does not work

Yelling, pushing, or greeting an excited dog with excitement of your own usually keeps the habit alive. Different family members reacting differently makes it harder for your dog to learn what is expected.

How Jacob teaches calm greetings

  1. Impulse control. Your dog learns to wait and settle before getting what it wants.
  2. A clear alternative. A place or sit becomes the way to earn attention at the door.
  3. Realistic practice. Guests, doorbells and family members come into the training so it holds up at home.
  4. Family consistency. Everyone gets the same plan at the handover.

Big dogs, small kids

A jumping Labrador or German Shepherd can knock over a child or an older relative. If your household includes kids or seniors, say so in the consultation so the plan reflects it.

Common questions

Questions owners ask.

Why does my dog jump on guests?

It is an excited greeting that has been rewarded by attention in the past. Consistent training gives your dog a better way to say hello.

Does kneeing or pushing the dog away work?

Usually not. Any physical contact or reaction can feel like attention and keep the habit alive.

Can puppies be trained not to jump?

Yes, and early is easier. Puppy Head Start builds calm greetings and household manners from the start.

What if my dog only jumps on certain people?

That is common. Share when and with whom it happens in your consultation so the plan covers those situations.
